# EV Charging Levels

Electric vehicle charging is generally split into three levels, distinguished
by power delivery and typical use case:

**Level 1** uses a standard 120V household outlet and the portable charge cable
that ships with most EVs. It's the slowest option, typically adding 3-5 miles of
range per hour — enough for overnight top-ups if daily driving is light, but
impractical as a primary charging method for most drivers.

**Level 2** uses a 240V circuit, similar to what a clothes dryer uses, and
requires either a dedicated home charging unit or a public Level 2 station.
Level 2 charging typically adds 15-40 miles of range per hour, depending on the
vehicle's onboard charger capacity, making it practical for a full overnight
charge from a low state of charge.

**DC Fast Charging (DCFC)**, sometimes called Level 3, bypasses the vehicle's
onboard AC-to-DC charger and delivers direct current straight to the battery at
much higher power levels. DCFC stations can typically add 100-200+ miles of
range in 20-40 minutes, depending on the vehicle and the station's maximum
power output, making them suited for road trips rather than daily use — frequent
DC fast charging is generally discouraged as a sole charging method due to
increased long-term battery wear.
