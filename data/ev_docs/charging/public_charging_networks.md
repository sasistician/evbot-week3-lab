# Public Charging Networks

For drivers without access to home charging, or for road trips beyond a
vehicle's single-charge range, several public charging networks operate DC fast
charging and Level 2 stations across the country.

Major DC fast charging networks include Tesla's Supercharger network (which has
opened to non-Tesla vehicles with the appropriate adapter in many regions),
Electrify America, and EVgo. Coverage and station density vary significantly by
region, with charging "corridors" along major interstate highways generally
better served than rural areas.

Public Level 2 charging is commonly found at workplaces, shopping centers,
parking garages, and hotels, often provided as a courtesy or at a modest hourly
rate. Pricing models vary by network and location — some charge per kWh
delivered, others per minute of connection time, and some require a
network-specific membership or app to initiate a charging session.

Typical costs for public DC fast charging generally range from $0.30 to $0.60
per kWh, though this varies by network, location, and membership tier —
noticeably more expensive per mile than charging at home on a typical
residential electricity rate.
