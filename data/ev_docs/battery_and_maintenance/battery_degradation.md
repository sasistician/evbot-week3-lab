# EV Battery Degradation Basics

All lithium-ion EV batteries lose some capacity over time and with use, a
process known as degradation. This shows up as a gradual reduction in maximum
range compared to when the vehicle was new — it's a normal part of battery
chemistry, not a sign of a defect, though the rate can vary based on several
factors.

Typical degradation for modern EV batteries is often in the range of 1-2% of
capacity lost per year under normal use, though this varies significantly by
vehicle, battery chemistry, and climate. Most drivers see a modest, gradual
decline rather than a sudden drop.

Factors that can accelerate degradation include:

- Frequent DC fast charging, especially charging to 100% regularly.
- Consistently exposing the battery to extreme heat, particularly when parked
  in direct sun in hot climates without active thermal management.
- Regularly keeping the battery at a very high or very low state of charge for
  extended periods rather than in a moderate range.

Many manufacturers recommend keeping a daily charge target around 80-90% for
long-term battery health, reserving a full 100% charge for road trips where the
extra range is needed, though this guidance varies by vehicle and battery
chemistry — some newer battery chemistries are less sensitive to this than
others.
