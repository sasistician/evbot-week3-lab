# EV Battery and Powertrain Warranty Coverage

Most EV manufacturers offer an extended warranty specifically covering the
battery pack and electric drive components, separate from — and typically
longer than — the vehicle's general "bumper-to-bumper" warranty.

A common industry baseline (partly driven by regulatory minimums in the U.S.)
is 8 years or 100,000 miles of battery and drive-unit coverage, whichever comes
first, though the exact terms vary by manufacturer. Some manufacturers offer
longer coverage on certain models, or on the battery specifically versus the
broader drive unit.

Battery warranties typically guarantee against defects and against
degradation below a specified capacity threshold within the warranty period —
commonly around 70% of original capacity, though the exact threshold and
whether it's stated explicitly varies by manufacturer. Normal, gradual
degradation within expected limits is generally not covered as a warranty
claim; the warranty exists to catch abnormal or premature capacity loss.

Buyers of used EVs should check whether the original battery warranty is
transferable to a subsequent owner and how much of the original coverage
period and mileage remain, since this varies by manufacturer and can
meaningfully affect the value of a used EV purchase.
