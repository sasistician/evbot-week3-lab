# State EV Rebate Example: California (Illustrative)

*This is a simplified, illustrative example of how a state-level EV rebate
program can work, using California's general approach as a reference point for
teaching purposes. Program names, funding availability, and eligibility rules
change frequently — verify current details with the state's official program
before relying on this for a purchase decision.*

California has historically offered point-of-sale or rebate-based incentives
for EV purchases through state-run clean vehicle programs, often layered on
top of the federal tax credit. These programs commonly include:

- An income cap, above which a buyer is not eligible for the rebate.
- A vehicle price cap, similar in spirit to the federal credit's price
  restrictions.
- Additional "clean vehicle assistance" rebates for lower-income buyers, often
  worth more than the standard rebate amount.

Some California utility companies and regional air quality districts have also
offered their own additional rebates on top of the statewide program, meaning
total incentive stacking can vary significantly by where a buyer lives within
the state.

Because state programs are often subject to funding caps and can pause when
funds are exhausted, buyers should check current program status before
counting on a rebate as part of their purchase budget.
