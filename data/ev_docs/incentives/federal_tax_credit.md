# Federal EV Tax Credit (Illustrative Overview — Not Tax Advice)

*This document is a simplified, illustrative overview of how the U.S. federal
EV tax credit has generally worked, for teaching purposes only. Federal
incentive rules change over time and vary by income, vehicle price, battery
sourcing, and other factors. This is not tax advice — consult the IRS or a tax
professional for current, authoritative guidance before making a purchase
decision based on incentive eligibility.*

The federal tax credit for new electric vehicles has historically been worth up
to $7,500, though the exact amount a buyer qualifies for can depend on factors
such as:

- Where the vehicle and its battery components were manufactured or assembled.
- The buyer's modified adjusted gross income, with the credit phased out above
  certain income thresholds.
- The vehicle's manufacturer suggested retail price, with separate caps for
  cars versus SUVs/trucks.

Some used EVs have also qualified for a smaller federal credit under separate
rules, typically capped at a lower dollar amount and with its own price and
income limits.

Because these rules and dollar amounts change with legislation, a buyer should
always verify current eligibility directly with the IRS or a qualified tax
professional rather than relying on general summaries like this one.
